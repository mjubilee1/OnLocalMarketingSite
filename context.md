# OnLocalAI — go-to-market context

This file is the source of truth for **who we sell to and how they arrive**. Use it when writing copy, CTAs, metadata, or page structure on this marketing site.

## How we get leads today

**Primary channel: LinkedIn.**

Almost all current interest comes from people who:

1. See a post, comment, or DM on LinkedIn
2. Click through to this site (often on mobile)
3. Decide in a few seconds whether this is for them
4. Either book a conversation or bounce

There is no paid ads engine, no inbound SEO funnel, and no partner channel that matters yet. Treat every page as a **LinkedIn landing page**: a cold (or warm) executive who does not already know the product.

The goal of the visit is **a demo / conversation**, not a self-serve install. “Get the app” is secondary and invite-only.

## Who we talk to

Primary buyers and influencers, in roughly this order of LinkedIn outreach:

| Persona | What they feel | What they need to believe on this page |
|---|---|---|
| **HR / People managers** | Onboarding is messy and inconsistent as the company grows. New hires get a different first 90 days. | This fixes ramp, consistency, and “where do I look this up?” — without another cloud HRIS project. |
| **CFOs** | Headcount is expensive; tools and cloud AI look like risk and recurring cost. | Data stays on our machines. No per-seat AI cloud bill. Clear why this is cheaper / safer than ChatGPT-at-work. |
| **CEOs** | Growth is outrunning process. They want one owner of the problem and a short path to a decision. | One product, one install, private by design. Book a demo — don’t make me assemble a stack. |
| **Founders / other entrepreneurs** | Same pain, smaller team. They still live in Slack, Drive, and tribal knowledge. | Fast to try, no servers, runs on a laptop. Privacy is a feature, not IT theater. |

Do **not** write as if the visitor is a developer, ML engineer, or IT admin first. Those people may evaluate later. The LinkedIn click is an operator: people, money, or the company.

## What the page must do for a LinkedIn visitor

In the first screen:

1. Name **their** problem (messy, inconsistent onboarding at fast-growing companies).
2. Show it is costly (ramp time, manager repetition, quality becoming luck).
3. Offer a **Book demo** path that feels like a conversation, not a download.
4. Signal privacy / on-prem so CFOs and CEOs don’t bounce on “another AI SaaS.”

Below the fold: proof of the problem, then the product as the fix. Feature depth is for people who already leaned in — not the hook.

## Copy rules for this audience

- Lead with the business problem, not the architecture.
- Avoid leading with “local LLM,” vector stores, sidecars, or model names.
- Privacy belongs in the story (“nothing leaves your machine”), not as a research paper.
- Prefer outcomes HR/CFO/CEO care about: consistent first 90 days, fewer repeated questions, no cloud data leak, one app instead of a pile of tools.
- CTAs: **Book demo** is primary. Download is for after they have a code.
- Assume the LinkedIn link preview (title + description) is the first impression — keep it problem-led and human, not “private on-premises AI workspace.”

## Out of scope for the homepage hook

These are real product capabilities. They should not compete with onboarding + privacy on a first LinkedIn visit:

- Timesheets, IT terminal diagnostics, command palette, role-play sandbox
- Installer / SmartScreen troubleshooting
- Stack diagrams unless the visitor has already bought the problem
