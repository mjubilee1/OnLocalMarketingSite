# Event Crew Training: Research Report

*Generated: 2026-09-23. Sources: 87 unique, cited in the template library. Confidence: high for US and industry practice, partial for the UK, none for Australia (see Gaps).*

This report is the basis for the Template library (`src/data/library/`). Every course and policy document in the app links the sources below.

> **Scope update (2026-09-24):** the library is now **US-only**. The UK template (Martyn's Law), the UK pack and the UK-charter accessibility course were removed. Universal courses were rewritten to US rules: OSHA noise (29 CFR 1910.95) instead of the UK Noise Regulations, OSHA ladders and lifts instead of UK work-at-height rules, US fire classes and banknotes only, and US breach laws (all 50 states, per NCSL) and CCPA instead of GDPR. Section 2 is kept for reference only. Two courses still cite UK *guidance* because no US equivalent was found: lost children (Cambridge SAG, Purple Guide) and de-escalation (SIA specification).

## Executive summary

- **Casual event crew have legal training duties in most US states.** Which ones apply depends on the role (alcohol, food, crowd management) and the state (heat, harassment). Deadlines range from *before the first shift* (Oregon alcohol, Utah) to 30–120 days after hire.
- **Several requirements changed in 2024–2026.** These include Oregon's alcohol permit rule, California's indoor heat rule, the 2026 FDA Food Code illness policy, PCI DSS 4.x terminal-tampering training, UK Martyn's Law, and the UK Crime and Policing Act 2026 spiking offence.
- **Existing training platforms have a clear gap.** SafetyCulture Training (formerly EdApp, **rebranded as Mitti on 11 Aug 2026**), TalentLMS, Trainual, 7taps, Connecteam and Rosterfy don't offer frontline courses on crowd-crush recognition, weather stops, festival welfare, lost-child procedures, live-event accessibility or radio discipline. The serious crowd-safety courses are paid, third-party and aimed at managers.
- **Templates are not certification.** Where a state or regulator requires an approved course or licence (server cards, food handler cards, SIA licences, crowd manager certification), the library explains the rule and tracks the certificate, but crew still need the official course.

## 2. UK (reference only: not in the library)

- **Martyn's Law:** the Terrorism (Protection of Premises) Act 2025, Royal Assent 3 Apr 2025. Standard tier covers 200–799 people and enhanced tier 800+, with the SIA as regulator. Enforcement is expected no earlier than about 2027.
- **Spiking:** the Crime and Policing Act 2026 (Royal Assent 29 Apr 2026) made spiking a standalone offence.
- **Noise at work:** the Noise Regulations 2005 set action levels at 80 / 85 / 87 dB(A) and have applied to music and entertainment venues since 2008.
- **Work at height:** the Work at Height Regulations 2005 apply, alongside the ladder guidance LA455.
- **Temporary power:** BS 7909 is the code of practice.

## 3. Industry best practice

- **Crowd crush:**
  - Still's standing limit is 5/m²; crowd turbulence starts from about 6/m².
  - Itaewon averaged about 7.5/m² and peaked near 10.
  - Deaths are from compressive asphyxia.
  - The Astroworld lesson: have a unified show-stop authority.
- **Weather:**
  - NWS: lightning can strike up to 10 miles away; wait 30 minutes after the last thunder.
  - Tents and marquees are not safe shelter.
  - Structure wind limits are set per structure (Indiana State Fair, 2011).
- **Welfare:** non-judgemental response, overheating vs. overhydration, recovery position (St John Ambulance).
- **Lost children:** stay in place with two staff for about 10 minutes, then go to the welfare point; never broadcast names; check photo ID at handover (Cambridge SAG, Purple Guide).
- **Accessibility:** the Attitude is Everything Charter (free companion tickets, staff training thresholds) and the Hidden Disabilities Sunflower.
- **Radio:**
  - Phonetic alphabet spellings are "Alfa" and "Juliett".
  - "Roger" means received, not yes.
  - Use METHANE for major incidents (JESIP).
- **Fire:** use PASS; extinguisher classes differ between the US and UK; every exit is for everyone (The Station, 2003).
- **Privacy:** data minimisation; UK/EU breaches must be reported within 72 hours; California CCPA rights.
- **Sustainability:** ISO 20121:2024 certifies the organiser's management system.

## 4. Recommended policy acknowledgments (in the library)

1. **Anti-harassment policy.** A NY requirement; IL bars and restaurants need one.
2. **Alcohol service policy.** One of the conditions for Texas safe harbor.
3. **Heat illness prevention plan.** Required in CA, WA, MD and NV.
4. **Food safety and illness reporting.** Required by the 2026 FDA Food Code.
5. **Emergency and evacuation procedures.** Needed for fire-code crowd managers and UK Martyn's Law.
6. **Cash and card handling.** Records PCI DSS 4.x training.
7. **Equipment authorisation.** OSHA aerial lifts and UK Work at Height competence.
8. **Data protection and confidentiality.** GDPR breach reporting and the CCPA.

## Gaps and unverified items

## Methodology

There were three parallel research passes: US law, UK/Australian law (stopped early), and industry practice plus a competitive scan. Between them they made about 230 tool calls, preferring official regulators, statutes and inquiry reports, with key sources read in full. Claims found in only one non-official source are either left out or labelled.
