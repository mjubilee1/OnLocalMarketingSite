# Cold outreach — KFC / Yum! Brands

## Before you send: three checks

1. **Is he at KFC?** My understanding is Scott Mezvinsky is a **Taco Bell** executive —
   Taco Bell International, then Taco Bell North America — both Yum! Brands, same parent
   as KFC. I am not confident he sits at KFC, and the `@kfc.com` address may therefore be
   wrong. Confirm on Yum!'s leadership/newsroom page before sending anything.
2. **Never assert his title.** The email below opens "Scott," and states no role. If the
   title is wrong the email still reads fine; if you add one and it's wrong, it's dead on
   arrival.
3. **Fit.** Our "compliance won't let us use cloud AI" wedge does not apply to a QSR.
   Drop it. What holds up for KFC:
   - Per-seat cloud-AI pricing makes no sense for a large hourly crew.
   - Ops and food-safety procedures are exactly the kind of high-stakes, document-grounded
     questions where a confident wrong answer is dangerous — and our engine cites the
     source and says "I don't know" instead of inventing.
   - Training consistency across constant crew turnover.

**Honesty guardrail:** no customers, testimonials, or results yet. Do not imply
otherwise, and do not invent statistics about KFC's turnover, store count, or operations.

**Structural note:** Yum! brands are overwhelmingly franchised. As with Chick-fil-A, the
person who actually buys tooling is the **franchisee**, not brand leadership.

executive at this level to pilot software.

**Subject:** crew training

Scott,

Most training in a KFC kitchen happens shoulder-to-shoulder — a shift lead walking a new
hire through a procedure while the line is moving. It works, but it means the version
each crew member learns depends on who trained them, and the ops manual mostly stays on
the shelf.

We built OnLocalAI for that. It turns a franchisee's own operations and food-safety
documents into something crew can actually ask questions of. Answers cite the procedure
they came from, and when something isn't in the documents it says so rather than
guessing. It runs on one computer in the restaurant — no cloud account, no per-seat AI
fees for hourly crew.

We're in early access and want a few franchise groups testing it properly.

Is there someone on the franchise-operations side worth sending this to?

## Recommendation

Send **B**. Brand-level executives at Yum! are not the buyer for a pre-revenue tool with
no reference customers, and a wrong-title email to a wrong address is worse than no
email. A regional multi-unit franchisee can say yes on their own, matches the ICP, and
becomes the proof point that makes a corporate conversation possible later.

If you do want the corporate route eventually, the sequence is: franchisee uses it →
documented result → *then* brand leadership.
